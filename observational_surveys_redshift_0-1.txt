Observational Galaxy Surveys (Redshift 0-1) with Group, Cluster, and Supercluster Labels
==========================================================================================

This list includes major observational surveys and value-added catalogs that identify environmental structures (groups, clusters, and superclusters) within the redshift range 0.0 to 1.0.

1. DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument)
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z < 1.5
   - Groups: Yang et al. (2021) catalog containing 5.1 million groups identified via a halo-based algorithm.
   - Clusters: Wen & Han (2024) catalog identifying 1.58 million clusters, including 338,841 with spectroscopic redshifts.
   - Features: Currently the largest sample of galaxy aggregates in the optical/IR regime.

2. SDSS (Sloan Digital Sky Survey) / eBOSS
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z < 1.1
   - Clusters: The WHL (Wen-Han-Liu) series (WHL12, WH15) identifies over 150,000 clusters up to z ≈ 0.8. The WH18 catalog specifically targets massive clusters in the 0.7 < z < 1.0 range.
   - Groups: Tempel et al. (2014, 2017) provide group catalogs for SDSS galaxies up to z ≈ 0.6 using Friends-of-Friends (FoF) algorithms.
   - Superclusters: Various value-added catalogs (e.g., Einasto et al.) identify superclusters (like the Sloan Great Wall) up to z ≈ 0.6. The eBOSS LSS catalogs provide tracer data for superclusters up to z = 1.1.

3. eROSITA (SRG - Spektrum-Roentgen-Gamma)
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z < 0.8
   - Superclusters: The eRASS1 Supercluster Catalog (2024) is the largest X-ray selected supercluster catalog to date, identifying 1,338 systems (including 520 "rich" superclusters).
   - Features: High purity due to X-ray selection, minimizing projection effects common in optical surveys.

4. GAMA (Galaxy and Mass Assembly Survey)
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z ≈ 0.5
   - Groups: The GAMA Galaxy Group Catalogue (G3Cv1, Robotham et al. 2011) identifies ~14,000 groups.
   - Features: High spectroscopic completeness (>98%), making it ideal for identifying low-mass "Local Group" scale structures.

5. VIPERS (VIMOS Public Extragalactic Redshift Survey)
   - Redshift Range: 0.5 < z < 1.2
   - Features: Targeted specifically to map the cosmic web and large-scale structures in the "redshift desert" around z ≈ 1. Provides catalogs of groups and density fields.

6. zCOSMOS (Spectroscopic Survey in the COSMOS field)
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z < 1.0
   - Groups: Knobel et al. (2009) group catalog reaching z = 1.0.
   - Features: High-density spectroscopic coverage in a small field, providing a deep look at group evolution.

7. XXL Survey (XMM-Newton Extra-Large Survey)
   - Redshift Range: 0 < z < 0.8
   - Superclusters: Identifies 35 rich superclusters using X-ray cluster detections.

8. 2dFGRS (2-degree Field Galaxy Redshift Survey)
   - Redshift Range: z < 0.3
   - Superclusters: Includes specific catalogs of superclusters and filaments for the local universe.

Data Access Summary:
- Optical/IR Catalogs: legacysurvey.org, sdss.org, gama-survey.org
- X-ray Catalogs: mpe.mpg.de/eROSITA
- COSMOS Data: cosmos.astro.caltech.edu
